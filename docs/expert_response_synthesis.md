# Expert Response Synthesis

## Bottom Line

The latest expert feedback reinforces the current direction:

**Keep VesperGrid. Do not pivot away.**

The strongest version is still:

> A critical infrastructure operational twin that uses AMD MI300X multimodal inference to perceive fragmented industrial evidence, structure it, simulate operational risk, and produce source-linked candidate plans.

The useful refinement is:

> Narrow the use case, but do not remove the emotional stakes.

So we should avoid broad public emergency-response language, but keep the demo urgent, tense, and visually memorable.

## What I Agree With

### Keep The Industrial Safety Wedge

Ports, logistics yards, factories, and energy-adjacent infrastructure are a better wedge than public emergency response.

Why:

- lower regulatory skepticism
- clearer buyer
- high economic pain
- easier synthetic scenario
- more believable operational constraints

### Keep The Stakes High

"Industrial safety" should not become boring. A solvent leak at a major port is not a sleepy inspection dashboard. It can shut down logistics, endanger workers, and escalate quickly.

The demo should feel urgent without implying that the AI is commanding public emergency responders.

### Limit The MVP To One Incident

The MVP should stay centered on:

**Sector 4 Solvent Containment**

Core contradiction:

- operator note says Gate 4 may be clear
- visual evidence shows route blockage
- VesperGrid catches the contradiction and recommends a safer candidate route

### Make Source Lineage The Main Wow Moment

The judge should be able to click a candidate plan and immediately see:

- source UUID
- source preview
- confidence
- caveat
- map zone

This proves VesperGrid is not hallucinating.

### Avoid Overclaiming GPU Simulation

Fake GPU simulation would hurt credibility. We should be honest:

- Qwen-VL inference is GPU-resident and AMD-relevant.
- Simulation is a lightweight MVP route/hazard layer.
- The product architecture supports deeper ROCm simulation later.

## Where I Push Back

### Do Not Make The UI Too Dry

The interface should be high-density and credible, but not bland. If we overcorrect into a generic enterprise dashboard, we lose memorability.

Decision:

- keep dark command-center mood
- keep heat orange and signal blue
- avoid crosshairs and military targeting vibes
- make tables/data sharper and more credible

The goal is:

> Bloomberg Terminal + industrial command center + AI evidence lab.

### Do Not Downplay Simulation Too Much

The expert is right that evidence-to-simulation is the differentiated thesis. Even if the MVP simulation is CPU/vectorized, it must be visible and reactive in the UI.

Decision:

- keep simulation visually prominent
- make it react to evidence and uncertainty
- describe it as bounded route/hazard scoring
- do not claim sophisticated GPU physics yet

### Preserve Bigger Vision In Docs

No live collaboration in the MVP. But collaboration can remain in the long-term product vision because judges reward ambition when the demo is grounded.
